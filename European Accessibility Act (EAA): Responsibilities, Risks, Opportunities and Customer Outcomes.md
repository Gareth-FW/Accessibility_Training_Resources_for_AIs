# European Accessibility Act (EAA): Responsibilities, Risks, Opportunities and Customer Outcomes
An AI Agent Training Document

## Purpose

This document trains a GPT to interpret and explain the European
Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882), translate regulatory
requirements into business and customer outcomes, and support
organisations in reducing complaints that could lead to enforcement or
prosecution.

The GPT should always frame accessibility as a **customer experience,
service quality, and market access issue --- not just a compliance
issue.**

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Core Concept: The EAA Is About Service Access

Customers do not complain because a guideline is not met.\
Customers complain when they cannot complete a task or receive an
unsatisfactory service.

Therefore:

-   Compliance risk = customer failure risk\
-   Enforcement risk = unmet user outcome risk\
-   Accessibility maturity = customer success maturity

The Act emphasises maximising foreseeable use, meaning products and
services must work for the widest range of users possible.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Scope and Responsibilities

## Economic Operators

The EAA applies to:

-   Manufacturers
-   Importers
-   Distributors
-   Service providers
-   Digital platform providers

It applies to organisations outside the EU if they sell into the EU
market.

## Covered Products and Services

Examples include:

-   Computers and operating systems
-   Smartphones and devices
-   Self-service terminals
-   E-commerce
-   Consumer banking
-   Transport information systems
-   E-books and readers
-   Telecommunications
-   Media services
-   Emergency communications

The EAA covers ecosystems, services, hardware, and user journeys --- not
just websites.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Organisational Responsibilities

## Product and Service Accessibility

Products and services must be usable without unreasonable barriers.

## Information Accessibility

Instructions, interfaces, and documentation must be accessible.

## Support Services Accessibility

Customer support channels must be accessible.

## Documentation and Evidence

Organisations must demonstrate compliance processes.

## Continuous Accessibility Management

Accessibility must be embedded into workflows.

## Operational Risk

-   Inaccessible systems blocking transactions

# Executive Summary

The European Accessibility Act represents a major regulatory shift that
reframes accessibility from a compliance obligation into a market access
requirement. Organisations that align with the Act can expand reach
across disabled customers, ageing populations, and users experiencing
situational impairments, significantly increasing potential market
share. By focusing on customer outcomes rather than checklist
compliance, businesses reduce complaint risk while simultaneously
improving satisfaction, loyalty, and trust. Accessible design also
drives operational efficiency by lowering support costs and improving
usability for all customers, not just disabled users. Companies that
integrate accessibility into product strategy are more likely to
innovate successfully and differentiate in competitive markets.
Regulatory alignment therefore becomes a catalyst for better user
experience, stronger brand reputation, and measurable return on
investment. Ultimately, the EAA creates both a compliance mandate and a
commercial opportunity for organisations willing to adopt an inclusive,
customer-centred approach.
