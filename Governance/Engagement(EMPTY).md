
As part of any accessibility governance framework there should be an engagement and outreach plan. These shoudl be used to spead the message publically about how the organisation is apporoaching accessibility in terms of putting its own commitments and policies into practice. An engagement plan has several orgnaisational benefits.
1. It can be motivating to staff, especially accessibility champions who have put extra effort into ensuring risk has been lowered, brand perception is improved, and both reach and engagement opportunity have increased.
2. It can build confidence with D/deaf, disabled and neurodivergent customers and representative groups, improving brand perception and increasing customer loyalty.
3. It builds knowledge, skills, capabilities and culture within the organisation.
4. It attracts talent as generally speaking staff stay with organisations longer and work harder if the organisation's principles and practices ethically align with their own.

There are several aspects to a baseline plan that should be considered:

## Events
It is important to enocurage staff, especially accessibilit champions and senior managers, to attand events and speak publically about the organisation's ethics, ambitions and achievements. This can get credit back to teams who have done the work, attract more talent to the organisations and improve brand perception. There are a lot of free events online which are a rich vein of ideas and free training. These include:

- ID24 is one of the best attended completely free online accessibility conferences
- CAN, Champios of Accessibility Network focuses on more of the challenges from a change menegement perspective and host monthly online meetups where members share best practices and ideas 
- GAConf is the main games accessibility conference
- GAAD, Global Accessiblity Awreness Day is a major event for every organisation's diary and is a great opportunity to celebrate success and access more training and development opportunities from the hundreds of events held around the world.

## Membership Organisations
For more mature accessibility and inclusion programmes membership of one or more organisations can build through engagement with peers and help with accessing support. The benefits should be weighed-up against membership costs.
- [Valuable 500](https://www.thevaluable500.com/)
- [Disability:In](https://disabilityin.org/)
- [Business Disability Forum](https://businessdisabilityforum.org.uk/)

## Lobby Groups and Charities
Holding an annual meeting with different lobby groups and representative charities can help build better public perception and also access data and feeback from orgainastions and their membership. This will usually come at a cost but that should be negotiated as it can also be utilized for publicity.
