# Guidance for Creating a Digital Accessibility Policy

## 1. Purpose & Scope
A strong policy begins by stating its purpose: ensuring digital products, services, and environments are accessible to everyone by removing barriers and facilitating inclusion.
Prompt questions:

What digital environments (websites, apps, games, events, social media, etc.) does your organisation provide?

How does accessibility fit into your wider diversity or inclusion strategy?

Who is covered by this policy (e.g., all users, specifically disabled people)?

## 2. Commitment to Inclusion
Accessibility should be framed as a core organisational value and linked to diversity, equity, and inclusion strategies.
Prompt questions:

How does accessibility align with your organisation’s mission and values?

What percentage of your audience or workforce may benefit from accessibility measures?

How will you ensure disabled people have comparable experiences to non-disabled people?

## 3. Governance & Accountability
Policies should identify responsible roles and reporting structures (e.g., Board-level champions, accessibility teams).
Prompt questions:

Who in your organisation is accountable for accessibility outcomes?

How will accessibility progress be reported (e.g., Board updates, annual reviews)?

Do you have an Accessibility Champion or network?

## 4. Accessibility Commitments
Outline clear commitments that guide product and service development:

Accessibility considered from the start.

Shared responsibility across Product, Design, Editorial.

UX design systems with accessibility built-in.

Compliance with WCAG AA, EAA, and platform accessibility features.

Prompt questions:

At what stage of product development is accessibility considered?

Which teams share responsibility for accessibility?

Which standards (WCAG, EAA, Section 508, etc.) will you commit to?

## 5. Support Structures
Define how accessibility expertise and resources are provided:

Dedicated accessibility team or supplier.

Guidelines, training, skills development.

Tools and resources (potentially AI-enabled).

Champions Network across projects.

Prompt questions:

Who provides accessibility training and resources?

How will accessibility knowledge be shared across teams?

Will you use external suppliers or internal teams?

## 6. Inclusive Research & Design
Accessibility policies should ensure disabled people are included in design and research:

Disabled participants in audience research.

Representation in consumer testing.

Feedback loops to improve products continuously.

Prompt questions:

How will you recruit disabled participants for research?

How will their feedback be incorporated into product design?

What mechanisms ensure continuous improvement?

## 7. Compliance & Continuous Improvement
Accessibility is not a one-time task — it requires ongoing monitoring and improvement.

Compliance with regulatory obligations.

Continuous research and iteration.

Reporting mechanisms for progress.

Prompt questions:

Which legal frameworks apply to your organisation?

How will you monitor compliance over time?

What processes ensure accessibility evolves with new technologies?

Note:
When drafting a Digital Accessibility Policy:

Start with purpose: why accessibility matters.

Define commitments: what you will do and how.

Assign accountability: who is responsible.

Provide support: training, tools, champions.

Include disabled voices: research and design participation.

Ensure compliance & improvement: standards and monitoring.
